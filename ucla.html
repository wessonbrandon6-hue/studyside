US university research board

GED pathway notes — confirm your exact qualification with each admissions office
CHECK BEFORE APPLYING
Stanford University — international applicants ↗Stanford says students need a secondary-school diploma or equivalent by enrollment. Its admissions materials also say a GED can serve as a high-school equivalent, but first-year applicants still need academic records/transcripts. Ask Stanford how your current GED programme and final credential should be documented.
Harvard College — international applicants ↗Harvard requires a school report and high-school transcript for first-year applicants and currently requires SAT or ACT, with limited alternatives in exceptional access cases. Its transfer application form includes a GED/high-school-equivalency option; that alone is not a guarantee of first-year eligibility. Contact admissions about your exact GED route.
University of Arizona — international admissions ↗Review international first-year academic and English-language requirements. Arizona's state university system recognizes certain state-recognized GED/high-school equivalency credentials, but confirm how a GED completed through your South African programme is evaluated.
MIT — international applicants ↗MIT reviews international applicants in the context of their educational system and asks for school transcripts and a school report. Its published first-year requirements include SAT or ACT. Ask admissions how to present GED coursework and credential evidence.
UCLA — international applicants ↗UCLA requires secondary-school records and country/system-specific academic documentation. Ask whether your GED credential and programme meet first-year entry requirements before relying on it.
UC Berkeley — international applicants ↗Berkeley says international first-year applicants must complete secondary school and earn a certificate that qualifies them for university admission in their home country or country of graduation. Confirm whether your GED credential meets that standard.

Important: Prep+3 describes your current programme level, not necessarily a completed GED credential. Do not assume the universities treat an in-progress GED course as a graduation certificate. Keep your course transcript, subject list, official test results and final credential records.
